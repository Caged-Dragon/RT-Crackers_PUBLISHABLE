5 interesting things you didn’t know about Diwali

Diwali/Deepavali is known for celebration, crackers and new clothes. We compiled these facts that people are often amazed to learn.

1. Which countries celebrate Diwali?

Of course India, but Diwali or Deepavali is also a public holiday in Pakistan (Sindh Province), Nepal, Singapore, Malaysia, Myanmar, Guyana, Mauritius, Fiji, Sri Lanka, Suriname and Trinidad and Tobago.

In Nepal it is referred to as ‘Tihar’ or ‘Swanti’. On the fourth day, the Hindu God of death, Yama, is worshipped for a longer life of the people.

In Malaysia, Diwali is celebrated as ‘Hari Diwali’ in the month of Ashwayuja. It is a national public holiday in Malaysia.

2. Is Diwali celebrated only by Hindus?

Diwali is recognised by 4 religions.

It is a significant festival for the Sikhs since 1577, as the foundation stone of the Golden Temple was laid on the day of Diwali.

Lord Mahavira attained his Moksha or Nirvana on the day of Diwali, so the Jain community in India celebrates the next day of Diwali as New Year’s Day.

The Newar Buddhists in Nepal celebrate the Diwali festival over five days, in the same way and on the same days as Hindus do.

3. When did fireworks come into the picture?

The earliest documentation of fireworks dates back to 7th century China (time of the Tang Dynasty), where they were invented. They were used to accompany many festivities, and eventually spread to other cultures and societies.

The first known use of fireworks for celebrations in India goes back to the 16th century.

Some of the biggest fireworks celebrations happen in the UK and USA: Guy Fawkes Day is celebrated in the UK on 5th Nov with a major fireworks display, and 4th of July, Independence Day in the USA, is a major fireworks event with big cities competing on the grandeur of the display.

4. Is Diwali a one day affair?

Diwali is celebrated as a 5 day festival.

  • Day 1: Dhan Teras (Dhanvantari Triodas), the official beginning of the Diwali festivities.
  • Day 2: Kali Chaudas or Narak Chaturdasi. In some parts of India, it is simply Choti Diwali, the day before Diwali.
  • Day 3: The actual Diwali/Deepawali, when Goddess Lakshmi is worshipped along with Lord Ganesha. People light diyas and candles in their homes, and the streets across India light up with millions of sparklers, crackers and fairy lights.
  • Day 4: Celebrated in various forms across India. In Western states like Gujarat it is celebrated with great pomp and show as Bestu Baras, the New Year as per their calendar.
  • Day 5: Bhai Dooj or Bhai Beej or Bhai Teeka/Tilak or Bhai Fota (in Bengal). Brothers visit their sisters, and the sisters prepare sweets specially in honour of their brothers, wishing them a long, happy, healthy life and great success.
5. Do people gamble during Diwali?

Yes, and it is found to be more popular in North India. Gambling during Diwali is believed to bring good luck and prosperity in the year ahead.

Dice games were popular in ancient India, and it has been suggested that Diwali, which also ended the harvest season, was one of the few times when a hard-working farmer had some money to spend and some free time, and could enjoy life and play dice or bet money.
